# The brief, decoded

## Verbatim

> In the world of AI, Trustpilot's Product Reviews offering is uniquely positioned to help consumers confidently evaluate products when making purchasing decisions and help businesses drive visibility of their products in LLMs.
>
> How would you evolve Product Reviews over the next 12–18 months to turn it into a high-impact product line for the business?
>
> **Strategic Direction:** Where would you place your biggest bets, and why?
> **Execution & Trade-offs:** What are the 2-3 concrete initiatives you'd prioritize first, and how would you handle the trade-offs?
> **Impact & Metrics:** How would you measure success and know the strategy is working?

## What the wording gives away

**"Product Reviews offering" — not "product reviews".** This is a named, monetised product line, currently an add-on on paid plans. The ask is P&L-shaped: turn a feature into a line of business.

**Two customers in one sentence.** Consumers evaluating products; businesses buying LLM visibility. Only one of them pays. A credible answer has to say how the free side feeds the paid side rather than treating them as two roadmaps.

**"high-impact *for the business*".** Impact is commercial, not usage. Bookings, ARR, enterprise mix, retention — Trustpilot is a listed subscription business with public guidance. (See `02-trustpilot-today.md`.)

**"12–18 months".** Two to three planning cycles. Not a moonshot horizon. Anything proposed has to be shippable and show a measurable signal inside the window.

**"uniquely positioned"** is a premise offered for testing, not a fact to accept. Trustpilot is #1 cited among review platforms — but Product Reviews specifically is the newest, least proven, most contested part of the portfolio. A strong answer stress-tests the premise before building on it.

## What good looks like against each criterion

| Criterion | Weak answer | Strong answer |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic Direction | "Lean into AI" | A named bet with an explicit thesis about *why Trustpilot wins this and not Bazaarvoice*, plus what is explicitly **not** being done |
| Execution & Trade-offs | A feature list | 2–3 initiatives with sequencing logic, a stated resourcing cost, and a real sacrifice named |
| Impact & Metrics | "Increase citations" | A metric tree linking a leading input metric → an AI-visibility metric → a commercial outcome, with a kill criterion |

## The hardest question to answer well

Trustpilot's AI citation advantage is **organic and uncontracted** — 99.5% of observed citations came from the strength of the content, not a deal. The strategy has to explain how that position is defended over 12–18 months when the model providers, Google, and a better-capitalised competitor with a Google partnership are all moving. (See `04-competitive-landscape.md` and `06-strategic-tensions.md`.)
