# Competitive landscape

Three distinct arenas are converging on the same budget. Trustpilot is strong in one, weak in one, and absent from one.

## Arena 1 — Reviews & UGC platforms (the incumbents)

### Bazaarvoice — the serious threat

The best-resourced, most directly competitive player, and the one making the sharpest AI moves.

| Move | Date | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Authentic Discovery™ API** | April 2026 | Makes brand and retailer reviews visible to AI search and shopping agents |
| **Shopify Certified Technology Partner** | May 2026 | Direct contest on Trustpilot's Shopify ground |
| **Google partnership** | May 2026 | **Verified reviews and photos piped into Google's Shopping Graph → Gemini and AI Overviews** |
| **AI Visibility Package** | September 2026 | Converts ratings, reviews and gallery images into AI-ready structured data |

**HarmonyAI** is the engine across the platform, sitting on **10bn+ pieces of shopper content**.

**The Google partnership is the single most important competitive fact in this landscape.** Bazaarvoice sends authenticated shopper content — verified reviews, high-res customer photos, review facets and attributes, refreshed every 24 hours — directly into the Shopping Graph that powers Gemini and AI Overviews. Google's Shopping Graph covers 50bn+ products.

Read it against the Seer data: **Gemini is the one engine where Trustpilot's citation rate is weakest (21% vs. 49–58% elsewhere)** — and it's exactly where Bazaarvoice now has a contracted pipe. Trustpilot's position is organic and uncontracted; Bazaarvoice's is a deal. Deals beat crawling.

**Bazaarvoice's structural advantage:** 1,750+ retailer syndication network. Reviews appear on Walmart, Target and retailer PDPs. Trustpilot's store-level collection model is structurally incompatible with those pages.

**Bazaarvoice's structural disadvantage:** it's a closed B2B network. It has no independent consumer destination, no "second opinion" brand equity, and its content lives inside brand- and retailer-owned pages — which is precisely the brand-owned content AI systems reach *past* when they want independent validation.

**And the disadvantage it shares with every other competitor: solicited supply only.** Bazaarvoice, Yotpo, Okendo, Judge.me and Loox can collect only what a brand asks for. A brand triggers an invitation; a customer may respond. **No invitation, no review.** Total possible supply is bounded by what customers choose to request.

Trustpilot has ~64m monthly readers, **100+ reviews posted every minute**, and North America organic reviews **+50% YoY** — people arriving unprompted to review a company nobody asked them about. That supply costs a brand nothing and isn't bounded by an invitation budget.

**None of them can copy it, because none of them has anywhere for a consumer to arrive.** A destination takes two decades and a consumer brand to build. It is the one asset in this market that cannot be bought, built quickly, or competed away — and at product level it is the coverage engine. *(See `07-practitioner-view.md` §4.)*

### Yotpo
Positions its reviews platform as feeding LLMs structured high-quality data for AI-first commerce. Strong DTC/Shopify footprint, syndication partnerships including Target, and interop with Bazaarvoice and PowerReviews networks. G2 support score 8.8.

### PowerReviews
High-volume handling and a broad retailer syndication network. The choice for brands that find Bazaarvoice too heavy and Yotpo too DTC-focused.

### The Shopify-native long tail — Judge.me, Okendo, Loox, Stamped, REVIEWS.io
This is where the *price* pressure comes from, and it is severe.

- **Judge.me:** unlimited product and store reviews, unlimited photo/video, Google rich snippets, 24/7 support — **$0–15/mo flat**
- **Okendo:** richest collection — attribute prompts, media-first forms, survey tie-ins — ~$119/mo
- **REVIEWS.io:** G2 support score **9.6** (vs. Trustpilot's 7.1), syndication to Google and retail networks, strong platform coverage

Against Trustpilot's **$799/mo Premium** plus a Product Reviews add-on, the value argument for an SMB ecommerce brand is hard. Trustpilot cannot win the long tail on features or price. It can only win on *independence, destination traffic and AI citation* — which means the April 2026 **Starter plan at $99** is the strategically interesting SKU, not the enterprise tier.

### Amazon, Reddit, YouTube — the uncontested giants
Not competitors for budget, but competitors for *citation share*. In AI Overviews studies, review sites and media appear in 89% of results, YouTube in 85%, **Reddit in 51%**. Reddit has a paid licensing deal with Google and OpenAI. Trustpilot is competing for answer-space against sources that are both larger and, in Reddit's case, contractually embedded.

## Arena 2 — AEO / GEO platforms (the new budget line)

A category that did not exist 24 months ago and has now absorbed **$472m** of venture funding across seven startups.

| Player | Position | Funding |
|---|---|---|
| **Profound** | Clear leader, Fortune 500 clients | **$180m Series D, Sept 2026, $1.8bn valuation** (~$334.5m total) |
| **Peec AI** | Best mid-market analytics; $4m+ ARR in ten months | $27.5m total |
| **Scrunch** | **Acquired by Sitecore, June 2026, ~$225m** | — |
| **Alhena** | Agent-focused | — |
| Semrush, Ahrefs, Otterly | Incumbent SEO suites bolting on AI visibility | — |

**Why this matters more than it looks.** Trustpilot's **AI Search Analytics** (April 2026) and **AI Search Market Insights** (September 2026) put it directly into this category — against a competitor with $334m in the bank and an enterprise sales motion built for exactly this.

Trustpilot cannot out-measure Profound. Profound measures *everything* — all sources, all engines, all prompts. Trustpilot measures the slice where it also supplies the data.

**But that is also the asymmetry.** Profound can tell a brand it is invisible in AI answers. It cannot *fix* it. Trustpilot is the only player that can do both: diagnose the visibility gap **and** own the highest-leverage lever to close it. **Nobody else holds both ends of that loop.** Sitecore buying Scrunch is the first move toward someone else assembling it — and a signal that the measure-plus-fix bundle is where the category is heading.

## Arena 3 — The platform layer (the one that can end the game)

### Google
- **Shopping Graph** (50bn+ products) feeds Gemini and AI Overviews — **Bazaarvoice has a direct feed into it**
- **Merchant Center product review feeds**: 50 total reviews, 3 per product minimum. Trustpilot is an approved partner — alongside Yotpo, Bazaarvoice, Loox and Junip. *No differentiation here.*
- **Seller ratings** requirements formalised September 2026: 100 reviews, 3.5 stars, 24 months, per country
- **September 2026: free product listings disappeared from Google Search across the EEA** — a reminder that placements can vanish by regulatory or product fiat

### OpenAI
- Publishes a **product feed specification** (id, title, description, link, price, availability, GTIN, brand, media, seller details, shipping) — merchants submit directly
- **OAI-SearchBot** crawls pages and reads schema.org markup live
- **Instant Checkout and the Agentic Commerce Protocol** (with Stripe) launched Sept 2025 and were **retired in March 2026** in favour of dedicated retailer apps inside ChatGPT — purchases increasingly finish on the merchant's site

### The structural point
Both major platforms now accept **direct merchant feeds**. A brand can submit product data to ChatGPT and Google without any review vendor. What a brand *cannot* self-supply is **independent third-party validation** — and that is the entire defensible core of Trustpilot's position. Everything else in the stack is being commoditised by the platforms themselves.

## Where this leaves Trustpilot

**Genuinely defensible**
- #1 cited review platform across ChatGPT (58%), Google AI Mode (49%), Perplexity (52%)
- An independent, open, consumer-facing destination — the thing AI reaches past brand-owned content to find
- 361m reviews, very high domain authority, ~64m monthly readers
- The "second opinion" brand, built over two decades
- Fraud detection at scale: 3.9m fake reviews removed in H1'26
- **Uniquely holds both the measurement and the remedy**

**Genuinely exposed**
- Gemini citation share is weak (21%) exactly where Bazaarvoice now has a contracted Google pipe
- No retailer syndication network — a structural gap in product-level distribution
- Product-level depth and price lose to Shopify-native tools
- Support perception (G2 7.1) trails the field
- Citation position is organic and **uncontracted** — no deal underpins it
- Formally disclosed as a principal risk: **"Reliance on search and answer engines"**
- Fake review submissions rose from 8% to 10% of all submissions YoY

## Addendum — the collection-layer race (Nov 2025 onward)

Added after a targeted check on review *collection* features, which the original sweep missed. This matters because collection is where Trustpilot's weakest link sits.

**Bazaarvoice has been competing on collection-for-AI for a full year.** Announced **7 November 2025**, positioned explicitly for "the age of AI-driven discovery":

- **Talk-to-Text** — shoppers speak their reviews; auto-transcribed and editable before submission. Framed as reducing friction to produce "longer, richer, more contextual content" that strengthens visibility in AI search.
- **Visual Content Coach** — guides shoppers on capturing impactful product images, with examples and creative guidance, to improve representation across AI discovery platforms.

Both auto-enabled for hosted clients on the SPSv2 submission form.

**Yotpo** ships a drag-and-drop review form builder and **AI Smart Prompts** that guide customers on what to write, explicitly to raise review detail and quality.

### Why this matters

Richer, AI-ready review collection is **not a novel idea** — it is a year-old competitive move that Trustpilot has not answered. Against Trustpilot's live form (two optional generic attributes, unprompted media, no variant capture), the gap on the collection layer is wider than the gap on the visibility layer.

The strategic read is not "we have a new idea." It is: **the category has already moved to competing on collection quality, we are behind on it, and we are the only player who can execute it on top of an independent company-level trust layer and a collection channel competitors don't have** (see `07-practitioner-view.md`, §4).

## Addendum — Trustpilot's product review display tooling

Trustpilot **does** offer a Product Reviews TrustBox that pulls reviews by SKU or product ID, alongside Micro Star, Micro Review Count, Mini Carousel, Service Review and Grid widgets. Shopify setup is supported via the enhanced app (GA June 2026) or custom HTML.

Widget access is tier-gated: basic TrustBox on Plus, advanced widgets (carousel, review highlights) on Premium. *Note: reported pricing for these tiers varies across sources ($259/$629 vs. $319/$799) — likely an annual vs. monthly billing difference. Confirm against Trustpilot's own pricing page before using a figure in the deck.*

So on-site display is **not a missing capability** — it's a competitiveness and packaging question. Ecommerce-native competitors ship stronger display tooling at a fraction of the price, which is the live trade-off captured in `../deck/01-strategy-spine.md`.
