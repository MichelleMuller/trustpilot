## 1. The stated vision: "Version 3.0 — trust infrastructure for the age of AI"

Blair frames the company's next phase as moving **from a review platform to "trust infrastructure for the age of AI."** Two expansion vectors:

- **Trust signals** — beyond reviews
- **Trust impact** — across consumers, businesses **and AI agents**

He explicitly names AI agents as a constituency. The strategy is not to compete with LLMs but to be *the infrastructure that lets them decide well*.

## 2. Open platform is a deliberate choice, not an accident

> "We're an open platform, we want to make the data as useful as possible to as many people as possible."

And on the consequence:

> reviews gain "a life of its own well beyond the Trustpilot platform itself"

**Results cited:** ChatGPT citations **up 400%**; Trustpilot now among the **top 10 most cited domains globally**.

This materially revises an earlier read in this repo. `04-competitive-landscape.md` framed the uncontracted, organic citation position as *fragile* — something to convert into a contract. Blair frames it as *the strategy*: openness is the distribution model, and the lack of a contract is the point.

Both can be true. Openness earns reach; it also means no floor. But the deck should engage with his framing rather than contradict it unknowingly.

## 3. Neutrality is named as the core moat

> "We're not a marketplace, it's not any kind of retailer. So we have no vested interest in whether a transaction happens."

Plus the architectural commitments: businesses **cannot delete critical reviews or remove their profiles**; reviews come down only if **proven fake**.

## 4. The other two moats

**Brand:** *"There just isn't that much room in people's heads for dozens more brands that represent trust."* Cross-industry positioning versus vertical players (TripAdvisor, Yelp).

**Data and fraud detection at scale:** 400m+ reviews, **200,000+ new reviews daily**, probabilistic fraud detection modelled on spam and credit-card systems. Scale makes the detection possible; the detection protects the scale.

## 5. The business model is fixed and explicit

- **B2B subscription.** Annual to three-year contracts. **83% gross margins.**
- **Advertising explicitly rejected**, despite the obvious temptation.
- Consumers use it free.

## 6. Enterprise, not SMB — stated plainly

> prioritised *"larger businesses where we just see such a huge need for C-level teams to more deeply understand trust"*

With *"amazing product market fit at the high end."* Focus on four geographies: **UK (~40% of revenue), US (~20%), Germany, Italy** — concentrated on enterprise rather than long-tail SMB.

**The buyer insight he names:** *"How well do you understand [trust]? Is there a team? Do you get reporting on it?"* Most C-suites have no structured trust metrics. That gap is the sales wedge.

## 7. The US is the headline growth story

US bookings **+27%** in H1'26, new business **+76% YoY**, after a refocus on *"customer segmentation, vertical go-to-market focus and empowering general managers."*

Strongest verticals: financial services, insurance, banking, credit unions, education, retail, software, hospitality.

**The ambition, quantified:** if the US reached UK penetration, it would be *"a billion dollar plus ARR business."*

## 8. On AI as an operating advantage

> "You always had this trade-off in business where you couldn't have all three of quality, cost and speed... I think AI is doing this thing now where you can have all three."

Also: AI-generated **review summaries** already help consumers digest volume — consumer-side AI is in play, not just B2B.

# What this means for our strategy

## Where we align — and can borrow his language

| Our position | His language |
|---|---|
| "Be wherever the consumer makes the buying decision" | Reviews gain *"a life of its own well beyond the Trustpilot platform itself"* |
| Product reviews as AI-consumable structured data | *"Trust infrastructure for the age of AI"*; trust impact extending to **AI agents** |
| Richer attributes beyond star ratings | **"Trust signals"** beyond reviews — our Bet 1 *is* a trust-signals expansion |
| Collection quality at low cost via conversational AI | *"quality, cost and speed... you can have all three"* |
| Free widgets, wider distribution of our content | *"make the data as useful as possible to as many people as possible"* |
| Don't support incentivised reviews | Reviews removed only if **proven fake**; neutrality as the moat |

**This is a gift for the deck.** The strategy can be presented as *executing the CEO's stated vision at the product level* rather than proposing a different direction. That's a much stronger position in an interview.

## Where we argue with him — and must do so knowingly

### Conflict 1 — Segment: he says enterprise, we say mid-market ecommerce

He is explicit: enterprise, C-suite, four geographies, *"amazing product market fit at the high end."* Our practitioner view (`07-practitioner-view.md`) says product reviews are acute for **mid-market and emerging ecommerce** — the segment Trustpilot prices out.

**This is a real disagreement and the deck must handle it head-on.** Options:

- **Reconcile:** company-level trust reporting sells to the C-suite (his insight holds); *product* reviews are a different product with a different buyer. Enterprise retail and ecommerce divisions are still the buyer — just a different function. The segment isn't "SMB", it's **ecommerce**, which cuts across his verticals (he names *retail* as a strong vertical).
- **Argue:** the Seer presence-cliff data says value is created at the lowest threshold of activity; our collection advantage gets brands over it fastest; the $99 Starter plan already exists as the vehicle. This is a genuine strategic choice with a cost.

**Recommended:** reconcile rather than argue. Position Product Reviews as serving the **ecommerce vertical** — which he already names — rather than as a pivot to SMB. Keeps the enterprise motion intact and avoids picking a fight with stated company strategy on slide 4.

### Conflict 2 — Neutrality vs. building a consumer shopping surface

> "We're not a marketplace, it's not any kind of retailer. So we have no vested interest in whether a transaction happens."

**Our Bet 2 builds cross-brand product discovery and comparison — with "Shop now" buttons on PRPs.** At what point does a product comparison surface acquire a vested interest in transactions?

This is the sharpest objection our strategy will face, and it comes from the CEO's own framing of the moat. The deck needs an answer. The defensible one:

> Discovery is not monetised on transactions. We never take a cut, never rank by commercial relationship, never accept payment for placement. Product discovery extends the *same* neutral service — "here is what independent customers actually said" — into product-intent queries. The moment placement becomes purchasable, the moat is gone. That constraint is a feature, and it's also why we can't fund this with ads, which he has already ruled out.

**Flag it explicitly in the deck as a risk with a stated guardrail.** Anticipating the CEO's own objection and answering it is worth more than avoiding it.

### Conflict 3 — "Open platform" vs. what robots.txt actually does

He says *"make the data as useful as possible to as many people as possible."*

But `08-prp-live-investigation.md` found `/product-reviews/` disallowed to **every** crawler — Googlebot, OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot — plus no product sitemap and no consumer product search.

**That is the gap between stated strategy and shipped product, in the company's own terms.** It reframes the finding from "a technical oversight" into "the flagship AI-discovery product is the one place the open-platform strategy isn't implemented." Much stronger framing, and it's constructive rather than critical — it's the easiest, cheapest fix available and it's fully aligned with what the CEO already wants.

*(Still needs the one confirmation noted in `08`.)*

## Corrections from the verified H1 results call

Where the verified transcript (`10-us-market.md`) differs from or sharpens what's above:

- **"Product reviews are not just for retail."** Blair explicitly extends Product Reviews beyond ecommerce to *"offerings"* — a bank's different accounts, TUI's holiday packages. This **resolves Conflict 1 below**: the segment answer is not "ecommerce *or* enterprise", it's ecommerce for collection volume and "offerings" for the enterprise verticals already winning.
- **Beta figures are firmer:** 400+ businesses, *"over 15X average increase in AI citation growth, generating 2.4 million ChatGPT citation requests."*
- **ChatGPT citations up more than 400% YoY** and **75x citation visibility** for active profiles — both confirmed in the call.
- **The attribution gap is acknowledged on the record.** Citation data comes from Seer plus Trustpilot's own server logs, which Blair calls *"a small subset of overall citations"*. On ROI: *"we are doing more... to help them really quantify the ROI. It isn't of course as straightforward as simply things like traffic metrics."*
- **The mechanism is stated by the CEO:** *"relevance, recency, ranking"* — and *"if your reviews are stale and many months old, then it's much less likely to get cited."* This is the strongest available endorsement of a collection-first strategy.
- **Domain authority of 100**, gross margin 83%, NDR 101%, GDR 87%.

## New facts worth using

- **ChatGPT citations up 400%**; **top 10 most cited domains globally** — stronger and more current than the Seer figures
- **400m+ reviews**, **200,000+ new reviews per day** (repo previously had 361m / 100+ per minute)

- **UK ~40% of revenue, US ~20%**, plus Germany and Italy
- **US: +27% bookings, +76% new business YoY** in H1'26
- *"A billion dollar plus ARR business"* if US reaches UK penetration — the size-of-prize line
