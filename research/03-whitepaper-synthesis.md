# The Seer Interactive study — what it proves, and what it doesn't

*Source: "What AI says about you", Trustpilot × Seer Interactive. Full PDF in [`../reference/`](../reference/).*

## The study

Commissioned by Trustpilot, run independently by Seer Interactive in **March 2026**.

- **804,491** AI responses analysed
- **1,926** US brands across **8 industries** (Money & Insurance, Shopping & Fashion, Travel & Vacation, Home Services, Health & Medical, Education & Training, Electronics & Technology, Business Services)
- **4 platforms:** ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, Perplexity
- Brands split into four cohorts by profile activity, benchmarked on Ahrefs data (brand search volume, domain authority, backlinks, organic traffic) to isolate the Trustpilot effect from underlying SEO strength

The benchmarking is the methodologically serious part — it's what separates "brands with profiles get cited more" from "bigger brands get cited more and happen to have profiles."

## The headline findings

### 1. Review sites are the #2 source category AI reaches for

| Source category | Share of citations |
|---|---|
| General web | 76% |
| **Trust & review sites** | **14%** |
| UGC | 5% |
| Editorial | 3% |
| Social | 1% |
| Data | 1% |

### 2. Review reliance scales with purchase intent

| Funnel stage | Review citation rate |
|---|---|
| Awareness | 2% |
| Evaluation | 16% |
| Intent (point of purchase) | **24%** |

**A 16x increase from awareness to decision.** Nearly 1 in 4 sources cited at the point of purchase is an independent review site. This is the single most commercially important chart in the document: it says review data is a *bottom-funnel* asset, which is where budget concentrates.

### 3. Trustpilot is the most-cited review platform on every engine tested

| Platform | Trustpilot | BBB | G2 | Yelp | Capterra |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | 58% | 15% | 7% | 0% | 3% |
| Google AI Mode | 49% | 14% | 15% | 17% | 8% |
| Gemini | 21% | 4% | 5% | 0% | 3% |
| Perplexity | 52% | 10% | 15% | 9% | 4% |

**Gemini is the outlier at 21%** — notably the one engine where a competitor has since struck a direct data partnership with the platform owner. (See `04-competitive-landscape.md`.)

### 4. The presence cliff — the most important number in the study

| Cohort | Definition | AI citation rate |
|---|---|---|
| No profile | No claimed profile | **1%** |
| Minimal profile | Claimed, ~13 reviews | **54%** |
| Active profile | Claimed, ~81 reviews | **75%** |

A **50x jump** from nothing to a claimed profile with ~13 reviews. The curve is a step function, not a ramp. Claiming a profile and getting a dozen reviews is the single highest-return action observed.

### 5. Small brands reach parity with mid-sized brands

Segmented by domain rating: smaller brands (DR 0–15) hit a **54%** citation rate with a profile, versus **53%** for mid-sized brands (DR 16–55). Review presence closes the AI visibility gap that domain authority used to create — in weeks, not months.

### 6. The co-mention effect — where scale still pays

**Direct visibility** (citations for your own brand) plateaus once a brand reaches the Active cohort. **Competitive visibility** — appearing when someone asks AI about a *competitor* — keeps scaling with review volume.

- Large brands with optimised profiles: **9.5x** more co-mentions than brands with no profile
- Emerging brands with active profiles: **4.9x** more

This is the real enterprise upsell logic: presence is cheap and plateaus, *share of the competitive answer set* is where ongoing spend earns a return.

### 7. Absence is read as a negative signal

When ChatGPT searched Trustpilot and found a profile, it cited Trustpilot in **90–96%** of responses. When it searched and found **no** profile, it still mentioned Trustpilot **34.4%** of the time — usually to flag the *absence* of independent review evidence.

Missing reviews are not neutral. They are evidence against you. This is the most commercially potent finding in the entire document and the sharpest sales argument Trustpilot has ever had.

### 8. How AI actually uses the content

Of **91,292** ChatGPT responses citing Trustpilot:
- **59%** referenced the TrustScore or star rating directly
- **16.6%** referenced the number of reviews
- ~3 in 5 surfaced a numeric trust signal of some kind

And when AI reads the review *text*, five themes dominate:

| Theme | Share of Trustpilot-citing responses |
|---|---|
| Customer service quality | 63% |
| Shipping and delivery | 34% |
| Product or service quality | 33% |
| Scam / legitimacy concerns | 30% |
| Refund or return issues | 21% |

### 9. Citations are organic, not solicited

**99.5%** of observed Trustpilot citations came from the organic strength of the content — the AI was not deliberately seeking Trustpilot by name.

## Trustpilot's own 3-step framework

1. **Establish presence** — claim the profile, automate invitations, respond to every review → 1% to 53%
2. **Build volume** — consistent collection, targets for volume/recency/coverage, measure AI citations → 75%
3. **Win the comparison game** — fresh high-volume base, track competitor prompts, shape narrative via review themes → 9.5x co-mentions

## Now the hard part: what this study does *not* prove

**This is a critical read for the assignment.** The white paper is the strategic foundation and it has four gaps that any serious strategy has to confront.

### Gap 1 — It is a study of *company* profiles, not *product* reviews

Every headline number — the 1%→54%→75% cliff, the 9.5x co-mentions, the 59% TrustScore reference — is measured on **brand-level profiles**. The assignment is about **Product Reviews**, a different, newer, SKU-level product. The only product-level evidence that exists is from Trustpilot's own announcements: a **12 ppt** brand citation lift and a **>1,500%** product citation lift from a *beta cohort*.

A >1,500% lift off a near-zero base is a true statement that says very little. **The product-level equivalent of this study does not yet exist — and commissioning it is itself a candidate initiative.**

### Gap 2 — Commissioned research, with the incentives that implies

Seer ran it independently and the Ahrefs benchmarking is genuine methodological rigour. But Trustpilot paid for it, Trustpilot chose the comparison set (BBB, G2, Yelp, Capterra — notably *not* Bazaarvoice, Amazon, Reddit or retailer-site reviews), and Trustpilot frames the conclusion. It is persuasive evidence, not neutral evidence.

### Gap 3 — Correlation is controlled for, causation is still assumed

Ahrefs benchmarking controls for domain authority and organic strength. It does not control for the possibility that *brands that claim and maintain review profiles are systematically better-run businesses* with better PR, better service and more web presence of every kind. The step-function shape is suggestive of a real mechanism, but the counterfactual isn't clean.

### Gap 4 — It measures citation, not consequence

Nowhere does the study connect a citation to a click, a consideration, a conversion or a dollar. **Citation rate is a proxy metric.** For 12–18 months of enterprise budget, buyers will eventually demand the next link in the chain. Whoever closes the loop from *citation → consideration → revenue* owns the category — and nobody has.

### A fifth, time-sensitive caveat

The study was run in **March 2026**. Since then: Bazaarvoice signed a Google Shopping Graph partnership (May), OpenAI retired Instant Checkout in favour of in-ChatGPT retailer apps (March), Sitecore acquired Scrunch (June), Profound raised $180m at $1.8bn (September). **Model behaviour and the source landscape have moved.** Treating a March snapshot as a stable advantage in November is the single easiest way to get this strategy wrong.
