**The market's view is less generous than the numbers.** Shares fell ~18% on the H1 print despite the growth, on a ~$1m net loss. That matters for the assignment: *growth alone isn't being rewarded; the market wants to see the AI story convert into durable, higher-margin revenue.*

### Scale of the asset

- ~361m active reviews on the platform
- ~100+ reviews posted per minute
- ~64m monthly review readers; trustpilot.com at roughly 80–113m monthly visits
- ~58% of desktop traffic is organic search — i.e. the consumer surface is *search-dependent*, which is precisely the channel AI is disintermediating

### Trust & integrity (the licence to operate)

- 3.9m fake reviews removed in H1'26 — **10% of all submitted reviews**, up from 8% a year earlier
- Two-hour posting delay on every review; models retrain weekly
- Business profile warnings +83% YoY
- A dedicated "Trust Capital Markets Day" held in May 2026

The rising fake-review rate is a live strategic issue, not a footnote. If review integrity is the product's entire value to an LLM, an adversarial increase in fraud attempts is an attack on the moat.

There is a packaging contradiction sitting in plain sight.

## Where Product Reviews is right now

Trustpilot shipped a significant Product Reviews expansion on **24 September 2026** as part of an "AI Discovery Suite":

**Product Review Pages (PRPs)** — dedicated, publicly indexed pages for individual products on trustpilot.com. Ratings, reviews and product detail at SKU or parent-product level; variants roll up into a single score. Launched across all English-speaking locales.
- Beta customers saw an **average >1,500% increase in product citations**
- Companies using PRPs showed **12 ppt higher brand citation visibility** than those relying on a company profile alone

**Attribute-level sentiment** — AI maps review text to category-specific attributes: sizing accuracy, material quality, value for money, durability, comfort, colour accuracy.

**AI Search Market Insights** — competitive intelligence: citation frequency vs. competitors across AI platforms, tracked over time. (An earlier **AI search analytics** feature shipped in April 2026 as the core AEO measurement capability.)

**Trustpilot AI (beta)** — an Enterprise assistant operating inside Slack, CRMs and LLMs; automates review responses, analyses trends, enables workflow automation.

**Location Reviews** — expanded bulk management and APIs for multi-branch retail networks.

**Collection & distribution rails:** Shopify (Trustpilot became a **Key Product Partner** in H1'26 with an enhanced native app), CSV catalogue upload, ecommerce integrations, Google Merchant Center product review feeds, on-site widgets, social share images, catalogue APIs.

## The structural weakness nobody at Trustpilot says out loud

Trustpilot's DNA is **company-level** reviews. That heritage shows up as three concrete gaps in the product-level game:

1. **No retailer syndication network.** Bazaarvoice pushes reviews onto 1,750+ retailer PDPs (Walmart, Target, etc.). Trustpilot's store-level collection model has historically been structurally incompatible with retailer product pages. PRPs are a destination play, not a distribution play.
2. **Thin product-level depth vs. ecommerce-native tools.** Okendo, Judge.me, Loox and Yotpo collect richer product reviews (media-first, attribute prompts, survey tie-ins) at a fraction of the price — Judge.me does unlimited product reviews at $0–15/mo.
3. **Support and price perception.** Trustpilot scores 7.1 on G2 for quality of support vs. REVIEWS.io at 9.6 and Yotpo at 8.8.

**The counter-asset** is equally real and harder to copy: an independent, open, consumer-facing destination with very high domain authority, 361m reviews, a brand that means "second opinion", and the #1 cited position in AI answers. Nobody else has all four.

## The risk Trustpilot has formally disclosed

In the H1'26 principal risks: **"Reliance on search and answer engines."** Trustpilot depends on external search, answer and AI discovery interfaces, and is exposed to changes in user behaviour, click-through patterns, *and the way third-party models access, interpret or display its content*.

That is the strategy problem stated in the company's own words.
